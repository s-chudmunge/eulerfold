Vitalik Buterin is a programmer and the co-founder of Ethereum, a decentralized, open-source blockchain with smart contract functionality. His work has focused on the development of decentralized computing platforms and the mechanisms of blockchain consensus.

### Early Life and Background

Vitaly Dmitriyevich Buterin was born on January 31, 1994, in Kolomna, Russia. In 1999, his family emigrated to Toronto, Canada. His parents were computer scientists, and Buterin demonstrated an early interest in mathematics and computing. As a child, he used spreadsheet software to design complex mathematical models and simulations.

In elementary school, he was placed in a gifted program due to his advanced mathematical abilities. He later attended the Abelard School, a private high school in Toronto, where he focused on logic and critical thinking.

During his teenage years, Buterin played *World of Warcraft*. In 2009, an update by the game's developer, Blizzard Entertainment, altered the mechanics of his character class. Buterin has cited this event as an early realization of the risks associated with centralized control over digital environments.

### Introduction to Bitcoin and the Creation of Ethereum

In 2011, Buterin learned about Bitcoin from his father. He began writing articles for *Bitcoin Weekly* and subsequently co-founded *Bitcoin Magazine* with Mihai Alisie. Through his work as a writer, he interacted with various developers working on blockchain technology globally.

Buterin observed that many developers were attempting to build specialized applications on top of the Bitcoin blockchain, which had limited scripting capabilities. He concluded that a more versatile blockchain architecture with a Turing-complete programming language was necessary to support a wide range of decentralized applications.

In late 2013, Buterin published the Ethereum Whitepaper, outlining the design for this new platform. Shortly after, he received a Thiel Fellowship and dropped out of the University of Waterloo to focus entirely on developing Ethereum.

### The Launch of Ethereum

In January 2014, the initial Ethereum core team, which included developers such as Charles Hoskinson, Joe Lubin, and Gavin Wood, gathered in Miami, Florida. Disagreements arose regarding the organizational structure of the project. While some members advocated for a for-profit corporate structure, Buterin argued that Ethereum should operate as a non-profit entity. 

In June 2014, a consensus was reached, and the Ethereum Foundation was established as a non-profit organization based in Switzerland. Following this decision, Charles Hoskinson left the project. 

### The DAO Hack and the Ethereum Classic Fork

In 2016, The DAO, a decentralized autonomous organization built on Ethereum, raised approximately $150 million in ether (ETH). However, a vulnerability in The DAO's smart contract code was exploited, resulting in the theft of 3.6 million ETH.

This event led to a significant debate within the Ethereum community. One faction argued that the blockchain's immutability must be maintained, meaning the theft should remain on the ledger ("Code is Law"). Another faction, including Buterin, supported implementing a hard fork to alter the blockchain's history and return the stolen funds to a recovery contract.

The community ultimately proceeded with the hard fork. A minority of users rejected the fork and continued to mine the original chain, which was subsequently renamed Ethereum Classic. 

### The Merge and Recent Work

For several years, Buterin and the Ethereum developer community worked on transitioning the network's consensus mechanism from Proof-of-Work (PoW) to Proof-of-Stake (PoS). PoW requires substantial computational power and energy consumption, while PoS relies on validators staking their cryptocurrency to secure the network.

On September 15, 2022, Ethereum successfully completed this transition, an event known as "The Merge." This update reduced the network's energy consumption by an estimated 99.9%.

Buterin continues to contribute to Ethereum's technical roadmap and writes extensively on topics such as cryptography, zero-knowledge proofs, and decentralized governance. He advocates for "Defensive/Decentralized Acceleration" (d/acc), promoting the development of technologies focused on privacy, security, and decentralized infrastructure.
