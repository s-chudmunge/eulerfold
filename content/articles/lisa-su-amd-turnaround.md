In October 2014, Dr. Lisa Su was appointed CEO of Advanced Micro Devices (AMD). At the time, the company faced significant financial and competitive challenges. AMD held substantial debt, its stock price had dropped significantly, and its existing "Bulldozer" processor architecture struggled to compete with Intel's offerings in performance and efficiency.

Upon becoming CEO, Su prioritized core engineering and product development over broader corporate rebranding initiatives. She directed the company to focus on building strong products, deepening customer relationships, and simplifying operations. Under her leadership over the following decade, AMD executed a major corporate turnaround, significantly increasing its market capitalization, capturing market share in desktop and server processors, and emerging as a primary competitor in the artificial intelligence hardware market.

### Early Background and Education

Lisa Su was born in Tainan, Taiwan, in 1969 and immigrated to the United States at age two, growing up in Queens, New York. She demonstrated an early aptitude for mathematics and an interest in engineering, often disassembling toys to understand their mechanics.

She attended the Bronx High School of Science and subsequently the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), where she majored in electrical engineering. During her time at MIT, Su worked extensively in the microelectronics fabrication cleanroom, gaining practical experience in semiconductor manufacturing. She earned her Bachelor’s, Master’s, and PhD in Electrical Engineering from MIT. 

### Career Progression and Management Philosophy

Su spent the early part of her career at IBM, where she contributed to the development of copper interconnect technology, which improved semiconductor performance. Transitioning into management, she developed a reputation for addressing difficult technical and operational challenges directly, a strategy she later characterized as "running towards problems." 

In 2012, Su joined AMD as Senior Vice President and General Manager, overseeing the company's global business units prior to her appointment as CEO.

### The "Zen" Architecture Strategy

When Su took over as CEO, industry consensus suggested AMD should diversify its product lines. Instead, Su decided to concentrate the company's resources on its core CPU business. She initiated the development of a completely new processor architecture, internally codenamed "Zen."

The development of the Zen architecture required a multi-year commitment of R&D resources during a period of financial instability. To manage this, Su implemented strict engineering goals, focusing on consistent, incremental improvements in design and execution, while enforcing accurate reporting of technical metrics. 

### Execution and Market Resurgence

In 2017, AMD launched the first processors based on the Zen architecture: the Ryzen series for consumer desktops and the EPYC series for data center servers. The Zen architecture delivered a 52% improvement in instructions per clock (IPC) over the previous generation, making AMD highly competitive with Intel in performance and power efficiency.

Su also reinforced AMD's "fabless" business model by strengthening its partnership with TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) for chip fabrication. This strategy allowed AMD to leverage TSMC's advanced manufacturing nodes while focusing internal resources solely on architectural design. 

Subsequent iterations of the Zen architecture (Zen 2 and Zen 3) further expanded AMD's market share. The company secured major contracts for cloud computing infrastructure with companies like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft, and provided the custom processors for gaming consoles such as the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X. 

### Expansion into Artificial Intelligence Hardware

As the demand for generative AI and high-performance computing grew, AMD expanded its focus to compete in the AI accelerator market, historically dominated by NVIDIA. Under Su's leadership, AMD developed and launched the MI300 series of AI accelerators, targeting data centers and large-scale AI training and inference workloads. 

By maintaining a focus on consistent engineering execution and strategic partnerships, Lisa Su successfully reestablished AMD as a leading designer of high-performance computing and graphics technologies.
