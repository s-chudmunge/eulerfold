Caitlin Kalinowski is a hardware engineering executive who has led hardware development at Apple, Meta, and OpenAI. She focuses on precision manufacturing, thermal dynamics, and mechanical constraints in consumer electronics.

Kalinowski emphasizes the physical realities of hardware engineering, noting that manufacturing errors carry significant financial and environmental costs compared to software development. She previously directed hardware teams for the Oculus Rift and Meta Quest headsets, and most recently led the development of Orion, a pair of 98-gram augmented reality (AR) glasses. 

In 2024, Kalinowski joined OpenAI to lead its Robotics and Consumer Hardware initiatives.

### Early Interest in Mechanics

Kalinowski grew up with a strong interest in physical mechanics, often disassembling remote-controlled cars and other toys to understand their internal operations. This interest led her to study mechanical engineering at Stanford University, where she adopted an iterative prototyping philosophy. She advocates for front-loading mechanical stress testing and early-stage failure analysis to identify design flaws before mass production.

### Apple and Precision Manufacturing

In 2007, Kalinowski joined Apple, where she served as a technical lead on the unibody MacBook Pro. The project marked a transition from assembling multiple plastic and metal parts to machining laptops from a single block of aluminum.

Kalinowski focused on fine stamping processes and tight manufacturing tolerances. Her work involved managing thermal dynamics to ensure the device dissipated heat effectively while maintaining structural integrity. 

### Oculus and Augmented Reality

Kalinowski left Apple in 2013 to join Oculus, shortly before its acquisition by Facebook. She managed the transition of VR prototypes into mass-market products, overseeing the hardware development of the Oculus Rift, Oculus Go, and Meta Quest series.

Her subsequent project, Orion, aimed to fit complex AR technology into standard-looking glasses. The glasses utilize silicon carbide lenses to achieve a 70-degree field of view while keeping the weight under 100 grams. The system offloads computing tasks to a separate wireless device and features a neural wristband that interprets muscle signals for interface control.

### Hardware at OpenAI

At OpenAI, Kalinowski directs the company's efforts to integrate artificial intelligence into physical devices, including robotics and consumer hardware.

She maintains her approach to product development, emphasizing functional testing over aesthetic polish in early stages. By building unfinished prototypes, Kalinowski aims to solicit objective feedback on functionality and identify mechanical issues early in the engineering process.
